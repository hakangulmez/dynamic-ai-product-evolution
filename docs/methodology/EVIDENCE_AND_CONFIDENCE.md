# Evidence and Confidence

## Evidence levels

### Direct

The source explicitly names the product/capability/task and describes what the customer can do.

### Strongly implied

Multiple official passages jointly establish the observation without a single complete sentence.

### Weak

Marketing or strategy language suggests the observation but lacks a concrete action or availability status.

Weak evidence may create a candidate but not a released observation.

## Quote requirements

Each released entity includes at least one short quote. Combined claims require enough quotes to support all material components.

## Confidence labels

- `high`: direct evidence, stable boundary, no conflict.
- `medium`: evidence sufficient but boundary or timing requires interpretation.
- `low`: plausible candidate requiring adjudication.
- `unknown`: not enough evidence to assess.

## Evidence coverage

A confidence score cannot compensate for missing evidence. The validator checks that the quote contains or clearly supports the action, object, and availability claim.

## Disagreement

Preserve model disagreements as separate candidate records until adjudicated. Do not average semantic labels automatically.
