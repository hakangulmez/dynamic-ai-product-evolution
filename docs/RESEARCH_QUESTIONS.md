# Research Questions

## Main question

How did software firms redesign customer-facing products and tasks during the frontier-LLM transition, and which combinations of frontier replicability, transformation depth, deployment scale, and task-specific defensibility were associated with different strategic and economic trajectories?

## RQ1 — Product and task evolution

How did firms' product families, products, capabilities, and customer-facing tasks change over time?

Observable outcomes include:

- new product introductions;
- product discontinuations and pivots;
- task additions, removals, splits, and merges;
- movement from manual or human-mediated delivery to AI-assisted or agentic delivery.

## RQ2 — Frontier task replicability

At each observation date, to what extent could the frontier general-purpose model available at that date satisfy the customer's underlying task without the focal firm's product?

This is a dated counterfactual. It is not the same as asking whether the firm's proprietary artifact can be copied.

## RQ3 — AI transformation depth

How deeply was AI integrated into the product task?

The project distinguishes:

- marketing or roadmap language;
- peripheral assistance;
- direct output generation;
- native workflow integration;
- multi-step action execution;
- agentic orchestration and exception handling.

## RQ4 — Deployment scale

How broadly was a transformation deployed across:

- product features;
- workflows;
- products and product families;
- customers;
- commercial plans and pricing?

Scale is not inferred from rhetoric.

## RQ5 — Task-specific defensibility

Did AI integration create durable differentiation, or did it merely repackage a capability available directly from frontier models?

Potential mechanisms include:

- workflow state and permissions;
- customer-specific data and integrations;
- proprietary or licensed assets that remain economically necessary;
- specialized production controls;
- execution capability;
- regulated authority;
- switching friction.

## RQ6 — Strategic archetypes

Which task- and firm-year archetypes emerge, such as:

- high replicability / shallow response;
- high replicability / deep but commoditized response;
- low replicability / deep differentiated transformation;
- protected but stagnant;
- task-destruction or portfolio-pivot paths?

## RQ7 — Operational and financial association

After the data are validated, how are task transitions and measurement profiles associated with:

- revenue and subscription growth;
- margins and operating income;
- R&D and sales intensity;
- customer or subscriber changes;
- employee productivity;
- market outcomes?

No specific causal design is precommitted at the repository's extraction stage.
